# Bogatyr: why not

**The graveyard:** what was considered for the layout on the body and why it is not used.

Each entry says what it was and why it went, so that nobody digs it up without knowing. Where a
thing waits in reserve, it says so.

## Left out of the EMG

- **The rotator cuff, the teres and the rhomboids:** surface EMG reaches them badly or not at all;
  wire is needed (Konrad, *The ABC of EMG*, Noraxon 2005, fig. 26). Surface supraspinatus and
  infraspinatus overestimate the wire signal (Waite et al., J Electromyogr Kinesiol 20, 2010).
- **The palm, the sole and the seat:** a grip presses the palm, the sole is stood on, the gluteus
  maximus' lower part is sat on, and pressed electrodes wander in the signal.
- **The abductor hallucis:** it varied between testers and sessions; the extensor digitorum
  brevis, the dorsal interossei and the abductor digiti minimi did not (Branthwaite et al., The
  Foot 2019).
- **The deep muscles,** the transversus, the psoas, the quadratus lumborum and the deep neck:
  wire only, though well-chosen abdominal sites follow some of them (McGill et al., J Biomech 29,
  1996).
- **The wrist and the ankle,** mostly tendons; the soleus' and the peroneus brevis' spots, which
  lie in the lower calf; the thumb's and the index finger's long muscles (the abductor pollicis
  longus, the extensores pollicis and indicis, the flexor pollicis longus), which arise from the
  middle third of the forearm's bones and lie deep (Gray's), the thumb showing in the hand's pairs
  and the IMUs; the masseter and the muscles that open the jaw, out of a headband's reach.
- **The hand's other three dorsal interossei** (to 3 Nov 2026): thin, under the extensors' tendons;
  the first, between the thumb and the index, is larger than the other three (Gray's, the
  Interossei). Taken back as a trial when the glove's eight channels were all laid out by the main
  muscle groups (the owner): what they tell under the tendons the recordings show.

## The layouts tried

- **Our own layouts, set aside for the professionals' advice** (4 Nov 2026): every channel by the
  main muscle groups on MakeHuman's average body, with its pictures and the script that drew them;
  then on Z-Anatomy's atlas two rows of 8 oblique pairs round each limb, and on the trunk rows of
  pairs, chords from one muscle to its neighbour, and chords scattered by a search over shifts of
  the suit. None convinced the owner: each try came out different, and the searches bunched the
  pairs where several muscles meet. We do not know how to judge a layout better without recordings
  or someone who measures EMG for a living, so the places wait for advice; what the tries taught is
  in the entries below.

- **Networks of crossed chords on even places** (3–4 Nov 2026): two rows of 16 places round each
  limb at equal lengths of its girth, chords three places on, the two eights crossing in X, a
  chord's run 3/16 of the girth. The crossing gave nothing: each electrode is in one chord, and what
  lets the model untangle the muscles is their overlap, not the X; places spread evenly, not by the
  muscles, put chords across one narrow muscle, which see little. The method took its place: the
  places on the muscles' bellies, the chords across from one muscle to the next, shingled.

- **Two rings round the thigh, the chords between them** (4 Nov 2026): two rings of 16 places 9
  cm apart, the places shared among the muscles by their widths, each chord from one ring's place to
  the other's next but one, 5–8 cm. A band's P and N lie on two lines 3 cm apart anyway, and at one
  height it reaches every muscle showing there; the second ring only doubled the places round.

- **One band of 16 a segment** (4 Nov 2026): 16 pairs side by side round each limb, their places
  closer over narrow muscles, the band tilted ~30° on the thigh, each limb's places and chord its
  own, written for the thigh, the calf and the upper arm with a table of sixteen chords each. The
  same day the owner's two rows of 8 took its place: one shape for every limb, which met the same
  muscles on the atlas and meets them however it is turned, so that how it sits on the limb does
  not matter.

- **The thigh's two rows 2 cm apart, then 16 cm apart** (4 Nov 2026). 2 cm apart at 16–23 cm above
  the knee, as on the other limbs, they reached the adductors longus and magnus one turn in three
  or two; set at 10 and 26 cm, rings of their own, they did better but were not the shape meant.
  The owner kept the two rows together and moved the upper 3 cm up: 5 cm apart from 16 cm, they
  reach the adductors in two turns of three. Lower down they lose them.

- **The band's electrodes in a zigzag or interleaved** (4 Nov 2026), tried on the forearm to keep
  the ulna's border free: every other P higher, or the N row slid into the P row's gaps. No more
  muscles were met, the zigzag laid neighbouring chords almost on one line, and the back of the
  forearm stayed empty all the same.

- **A band tilted 45° on the thigh** (4 Nov 2026): the strip keeps its shape, so its chords turn
  with it, on the outer back nearly along the fibres and in front leaning the other way, and its
  electrodes come within 1.7 cm; it met fewer muscles well than at 30°.

- **A tilted band on the calf** (4 Nov 2026): tilts of 15–45° met the calf's muscles no better
  than a level band; they run straight down, and the gastrocnemius covers most of the back.

- **A band low on the upper arm** (4 Nov 2026): the atlas scored it best 6–7 cm above the
  epicondyles, where the brachialis and the triceps' medial head show wider, every muscle there
  twice well inside; but there the elbow folds, the forearm pressing the arm's front when it bends
  fully (an inference), and the IMUs and the bias over the lateral ridge sit. On a body the
  band goes about the arm's middle.

- **Pairs from one muscle each** (25 Oct to 3 Nov 2026): the first eight on named muscles at
  SENIAM's spots, each N 4–8 cm along the fibres or across by turns, the second eight as rings and
  crossings on the same muscles. Half the channels were long pairs on one muscle, half oblique,
  neither the classic pair nor the network the suit was meant for (the owner). The model by regions
  took its place: networks of crossed chords on the limbs and across the trunk's regions, scaled to
  the body, short pairs on muscles only on the hand, the foot and the head.

- **The head's second eight in the hair** (3 Nov 2026): the temporalis' middle and back high
  above the ear, the occipitalis at the back half's sides, their N higher still. It broke what
  was agreed for the head, nothing in the hair, at most the edges shaved; the occipitalis, thin
  and ending in the galea (Gray's), tells a controller little besides. The frown and the eye's
  closing took their places, the temporalis' middle moved down above the ear.

- **The second eight left open** (to 3 Nov 2026): every module had its first eight, the forearm
  and the calf five, the hand and the foot three, the rest to be laid out with someone who knows
  the body. None was to be had, and every channel was laid out by the main muscle groups instead,
  the second eight as rings and crossings on the same muscles, the first recordings to check it.
- **Two rings a limb, each P to the N half a turn round** (to 25 Oct 2026), every channel running
  obliquely through the limb, the crossing called "tomography" in quotes, and the torso's units as
  areas whose chords crossed from one half to the other. Long pairs gather more crosstalk (De Luca
  et al., J Biomech 45, 2012) and, on the trunk, more of the heart (Nedios et al., J Electrocardiol
  47, 2014); the owner's layout of 25 Oct keeps the pairs at 4–8 cm and crosses only the spine,
  diagonally. That layout's picture and its script went with it.
- **A single ring of sixteen,** scored by a script of ours, gone with it, over every pairing on a
  model limb: neighbours told the muscles apart best but changed by half when the band moved ~1 cm,
  chords across the limb least. The model was a sketch, not anatomy; the ring gave way to two rings,
  and those to the owner's layout.
- **A reference electrode not fitted on the suit** (to 25 Oct 2026), the room for one kept on
  every module; then one on every module (to 29 Oct 2026). The body's ADS1298 have no SRB1, so
  the reference stayed on the head and the neck, where an ADS1299's SRB1, switched by its MISC1,
  gave both ways on one strip. With the ADS1298 there too (31 Oct 2026, see
  [Rubaška](../rubaska/WHY.md#the-sensing-module)), the strip wires the reference to the
  channels' negative inputs itself, and which way serves the model better was to be tried there.
  Dropped on 3 Nov 2026: the owner measures differentially everywhere, a reference being too crude
  for the suit, and the head and the neck lose their reference electrode, 580 electrodes in all. A
  module wired against a reference on the first arm, to form every pair in software from one
  recording, went with it the same day; two strips are compared instead.
- **The foot behind the calf's module and the hand behind the forearm's,** an extension of up to
  four IMUs on a processor of its own (to 25 Oct 2026), then **two IMUs beyond the joint on the
  calf's and the forearm's module's strip** (to 29 Oct 2026). The hand and the foot want more than
  a module's four places, losing their detail making no sense (the owner), so the sock, Nosok, a
  unit of the glove's kind in the limb's fourth slot, carries up to eight and the glove 20 (see
  [Rubaška](../rubaska/HARDWARE.md#the-sock)).

## The places weighed

- **Electrodes on the palm.** Every grip presses them and they wander. The hand keeps its edges,
  the first web and the back of the hand, where the extensors' tendons cover all but the first
  dorsal interosseous, the other three being a trial.
- **Rings round the chest and the waist,** two modules each. A ring takes the torso at one
  height only; the torso's modules take areas, the neck to the skull, the shoulders, the front,
  the lower back and the hips, each muscle with its pair.
- **The deltoid on the upper arm's module.** The shoulder has a module of its own; the upper
  arm keeps a ring of eight with their N.
- **One ring on a limb with its pairs square across the fibres.** It crosses the fibres only;
  pairs along and across by turns held up better when electrodes shifted (Young, Hargrove and
  Kuiken 2012). The limbs' bands now are two rows of pairs leaning 30° off the square.
- **A module on the hand** (to 29 Oct 2026): its three pairs went across the wrist to the
  forearm's module, which kept a ring of five with their N, the hand carrying only the glove's
  IMUs. With sixteen channels a module the forearm has its own eight and more, and the glove's
  board carries an ADS1298 for the hand's eight, those three pairs among them (see
  [Rubaška](../rubaska/WHY.md#the-sensing-module)).
- **A chin strap for the head's band,** from the temple over the masseter, under the chin and
  up the other side, to see the jaw close and open. Nobody wants to wear a dog's muzzle; a
  headband sees the temporalis, which closes the jaw, and the IMUs and the neck do the rest.
- **The neck's ring at the middle of the neck.** It would sit on the division of the common
  carotid, opposite the upper border of the thyroid cartilage, where pressure on the carotid
  sinus can slow the heart; the neck's electrodes in front keep below it, and the neck sits
  loose.
- **A narrow strap under the jaw, above the larynx.** It would lie on the carotid's division,
  at the larynx's upper border, where the vessels are superficial (Gray's, the carotid
  triangle), and ride on the hyoid, which the muscles under the jaw lift in swallowing and
  pull on to open the mouth.
- **A full balaclava as the head's part,** closed under the chin. It would bring the masseter
  under the fabric, but its electrodes want a cheek shaved every day, of whoever wears the
  suit; the headband and the turtleneck do without. It stays an option.
